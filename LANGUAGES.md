# Languages of Gielinor

Reference for `speech.json`: what the OSRS Wiki attests about each in-world language, researched 2026-10-12. Anything from the RS3 wiki is marked **[RS3]** and should be used with care. Only words listed here go into lines.

The OSRS `Category:Languages` has exactly 9 pages: **Ancient Gnomish, Dragonkin language (redirects to Dragonkin#Language), Lunar alphabet, Monkey language, Old Ones language (redirects to Old Ones#Language), Skavid language (redirects to Skavid#Language), Tzhaar language (redirects to TzHaar#Language), Vampyre language (redirects to Vampyre#Language), VPSG code**. There is no separate "Elder language" page; the wiki calls it the **Old Ones language**. Elvish, Kharidian, Fremennik, Dwarven, Goblin, Ogre, Varlamore and Mahjarrat are not in the OSRS category. What the wiki says about them is in the "Other languages" section at the end.

**Main rule:** most races speak the common tongue in OSRS. Their own languages show up as single words, names, compounds and short exclamations. That is the right scale for sprinkling words into NPC speech.

## 1. Ancient Gnomish

- **Speakers / where:** the gnome race, historically. Today it is "no longer widely spoken; gnomes have mostly forgotten it". Gnome glider pilots still name their destinations in Gnomish. **Hazelmere** (an island east of Yanille) is the only gnome who speaks almost only Gnomish, and he does it by choice: he finds the common tongue "rudimentary" and "uncivilised". **Anita** (the Grand Tree) translates it. Gnome children sometimes say a Gnomish line. Glough uses it in Monkey Madness II.
- **Can the player understand it?** Not directly. In The Grand Tree you translate with the **translation book** from King Narnode. Hazelmere's untranslated speech shows as "Blah, blah..." until he uses a mind-meld (blue text).

### Attested sentences
- Gnome child: **"Es ataris eto meriz ip prit es gutus!"** = "A cow will kill you with a banana!"
- Hazelmere's scroll (The Grand Tree): **"Es lemanto meso pro eis prit ta Cinqo mond. Mi lovos te lemanto Daconia arpos et Daconia arpos eto meriz ta priwl!"** = "A man came to me with the King's seal. I gave the man Daconia rocks and Daconia rocks will kill the tree!"

**Usage notes:** word order is close to English: subject, verb, object, with prepositions. Good for sprinkling: insults ending in `-olly` (gnomes calling humans *lemantolly*), "kar!" (no), "sarko!" (begone), "ta Quir Priw", "Cinqo Narnode". Old or grumpy gnomes are the most in-character speakers.

## 2. Dragonkin language

- **Speakers / where:** the dragonkin. In OSRS they speak the common tongue in their few interactions. The **only OSRS in-game example** is a **shrine in the northern Wyvern Cave** (Fossil Island). Related lore: Forcae (creator of adamant and rune dragons), Lithkren (fortress and vault), Galvek (Dragon Slayer II).
- **Source caveat:** the wiki says most words "were taken from RS3's *Dragonkin Primer* or other RS3 texts, which Old School Jagex Moderators have used in related content". In practice the vocabulary is shared between the two games but comes from RS3.
- **Grammar:** "direct and simple". Order is **Subject, Adjective (if needed), Action modifier, Action, Object**.
- **Can the player understand it?** Only the shrine examine text, which the wiki translates. No NPC speaks it in OSRS.

### Attested sentence (OSRS)
"**Forcae castil draekeun. Ortha lokur. Lith kletter ortha lokur. Draekeun fia Lithkren.**" = *Forcae combine dragon. First steel. Superior green first steel. Dragon safe from Lithkren.*

## 3. Lunar ("Moon Clan language"): an alphabet only, no vocabulary

- **What the wiki has:** the **Lunar alphabet**, a 26-letter cipher with one symbol per Latin letter. It is **writing only; no spoken Lunar words are attested**. It is used on **Zanaris** and **Lunar Isle**. It appears on the Cosmic Altar's rune temple sign ("COSMIC RUNE ALTAR"), on Fairy Nuff's hidden message (Fairytale II), and as the jinx symbols a Moon Clan girl taught the Lady Zay's cabin boy (Lunar Diplomacy). Most Zanaris residents don't recognise it ("doodles", "Rorschach ink-blots"). The name is not used in-game; it comes from the game's code.
- **Moon Clan:** magical humans on Lunar Isle. They **speak the common tongue**.
- **Can the player understand them?** Yes. The **Seal of passage** is a *permission* token, not a translator. Without it, talking to any Lunar Isle NPC teleports you back to Rellekka; the Fremennik Elite diary removes the need for it. There is nothing to translate.
- **Sprinkling:** there are no words to use. Moon Clan flavour has to come from the moon, dreams, Lunar magic and the Fremennik rivalry, not from vocabulary. Do not invent Lunar words.

## 4. Monkey language

- **Speakers / where:** monkeys. There are three variants:
  1. **Ape Atoll (Marim) and the Ardougne Zoo:** both use the same generative "ook" language.
  2. **Mos Le'Harmless** native monkeys: a simplified form.
  3. **Kharidian Desert** magic-carpet monkeys: a distinct variant.
- **Can the player understand it?** Only while wearing the **M'speak (monkeyspeak) amulet**, or after **completing Monkey Madness II**, which removes the need for it. Otherwise it shows as babble. You get the amulet during Monkey Madness I; it is also used in the RFD Awowogei subquest and MM2.

### Ape Atoll and Zoo phrase pool (exact)
`Ah Ah!` · `Ah Uh Ah!` · `Ah!` · `Ook Ah Ook!` · `Ook Ah Uh!` · `Ook Ook!` · `Ook!` · `Ook.`

**The game's own generator:** pick phrases at random and join them with spaces until the line contains 8 or more spaces (there is always a trailing space). Example: "Ook Ah Ook! Ook. Ah Ah! Ook! Ook Ah Uh! ".

```python
while dialogue.count(" ") < 8:
    dialogue += random.choice(phrases) + " "
```
The Monkey's Aunt uses three such sentences. The "Listen" option on the monkey dentures and the enchanted bar uses the same pool.

### Mos Le'Harmless
- "Eeekeek ookeek!" (normal talk in the jungle)
- "Eeek!  Eeek!  Ook!" (during Trouble Brewing)

### Kharidian Desert (magic carpet monkeys)
- "Ukkuk oook! Eeek aka, ahh aka gonk." (normal)
- "Uruk aka oook! Eeek aka, ahh aka ook." (when given a banana)

**Notes:** there is no word-to-meaning vocabulary; the speech is pure babble. Marimbo is the monkeys' god, not a language. For immersion, monkeys should "ook" unless the plugin can check MM2 completion or an equipped M'speak amulet. If it can, show plain English. Stringing the amulet makes "a slight 'Ook' sound", which is a good bit of flavour.

## 5. Old Ones language (the Varlamore "elder" language)

- **Speakers / where:** the **Old Ones**, an extinct ancient race of **Varlamore** and the **Kebos Lowlands**. Their sites include Neypotzli (Cam Torum, Perilous Moons), Tapoyauik, the ritual site and Stranglewood near Mount Quidamortem, and Mokhaiotl. The language survives in **ruins, stone tablets and spirit-guardians**. **Eyatlalli** (Neypotzli) uses "iknil" when addressing the player. **Prince Itzla Arkan** translates words during The Heart of Darkness and The Final Dawn. The Alchemical Society and Varlamore religion (Losanium, e.g. *Ximoua*) inherit its terms.
- **Source:** the **Word translations** scroll from the stone tablet south-east of Mount Quidamortem (The Garden of Death), plus Prince Itzla.
- **Usage note:** most words are generic and have **several context-dependent meanings** ("miki" = time / death / die / kill). This fits cryptic, ritual-sounding speech.
- **Can the player understand it?** Through the translations scroll and translator NPCs. No common NPC speaks it fluently.

## 6. Skavid language

- **Speakers / where:** **skavids**, small, grey, clawed humanoids enslaved by the ogres of **Gu'Tanoth**. They live in the **Skavid caves in the Feldip Hills**. Only 6 are known: the Scared skavid, four Skavids, and the Mad skavid, all from the Watchtower quest. They also speak the human tongue, using Skavid "for safety, so their cruel Ogre masters can't understand".
- **Can the player understand it?** During **Watchtower** the Scared skavid teaches "the most common skavid words: **Ar, nod, gor, ig and cur**", and you learn replies from the others. **The wiki gives no meanings for any Skavid word.** Do not assign meanings.

### Vocabulary (meanings unattested)
`Ar` · `Bidith` · `Cur` · `Gor` · `Ig` · `Nod` (also "Nods") · `Tanath`

The **[RS3]** Languages page adds that skavids were the indigenous race of the Feldip Hills, driven almost extinct by Bandosians in the Third Age.

## 7. TzHaar language

- **Speakers / where:** the **TzHaar** of **Mor Ul Rek** (TzHaar City, under the Karamja Volcano: Fight Caves, Fight Pit, Inferno). Also the **Tasakaal** at Mount Karuulm, prefix *Kaal*. The race is "TzHaar" = sacred fire.
- **In-game use:** TzHaar **mostly speak the common tongue in broken, article-dropping English** and mix in TzHaar nouns and names. Examples: "Silly JalYt, I am TzHaar-Hur, one of the crafters for this city." "JalYt, we need help, but this is extremely dangerous. TzHaar not strong enough to take control back." "What ever you say, crazy JalYt!" "Luck be a TzHaar tonight. Jal-Nib-Rek is yours."
- **Language nature:** words are "more about the emotion and passion behind the word" than literal meaning. Mostly nouns and adjectives, very few verbs. Words **combine into compounds**, written with hyphens or run together (JalYt).
- **Source caveat:** the vocabulary comes mostly from **[RS3]'s TzHaar Tourist Guide**, and the wiki notes OSRS Jagex Mods use it in TzHaar content.
- **Can the player understand it?** No translation item is needed; NPCs translate as they go. Players learn the words from context.

### Names and addressing the player (OSRS)
Structure: **Race - Caste - Honorific - Given name**. NPC dialogue uses the template "[TzHaar caste]-[player name]" or "JalYt-[caste]-Xo-[player name]".
- **Race:** `JalYt` (human), `TzHaar`, `Kaal` (Tasakaal).
- **Caste:** chosen by the highest skill pair. **Mej** = Magic+Prayer, **Ket** = Strength+Defence, **Xil** = Attack+Agility, **Hur** = Crafting+Smithing. Tie order: Mej, Ket, Xil, Hur.
- **Honorific:** `Xo` after showing a fire cape to a TzHaar-Ket (inner city access); `Kal` after defeating TzKal-Zuk.
- **NPC castes:** TzHaar-Ket (guards; run the Fight Caves, Fight Pit and Inferno), TzHaar-Xil (hunters), TzHaar-Mej (mystics, guides), TzHaar-Hur (crafters). Named NPCs follow the pattern TzHaar-Caste-Name (TzHaar-Ket-Keh, TzHaar-Hur-Tel, TzHaar-Mej-Jal).

**Sprinkling notes:** this is the best-supported OSRS language for ambient lines. Use "JalYt" for the player, TokKul for money, broken grammar with dropped articles ("JalYt want TokKul?"), and caste words. With the plugin's skill data, a TzHaar could address the player correctly as "JalYt-Ket-Name".

## 8. Vampyre (Vampyric) language

- **Speakers / where:** vampyres of **Morytania**: **Darkmeyer**, **Meiyerditch**, the Sanguinesti region, Ver Sinhaza. Per **Flaygian Screwte** (A Taste of Hope): "very old and they don't use it much anymore", useful "where they don't want humans prying into their work".
- **Attested text:** **none readable**. "No examples of it can be seen in-game." *Haemalchemy volume 1* and *volume 2* are "mostly written in the vampyric language", but they are not transcribed or translated.
- **Vampyric names (OSRS):** Vanescula Drakan gives the player one in Sins of the Father, and Darkmeyer vampyres use it. These are names, not vocabulary:
  - First names: Anaric, Balnar, Carnivar, Dracona, Epsenti, Haemar, Kaerin, Leardin, Molark, Morytan, Noctil, Remian, Rumelo, Sanguinak, Vanpyrus, Varniprus
  - Last names: Caermoran, Caernivus, Constantine, Diaemus, Draskarin, Helsing, Jargarin, Kardava, Lasarin, Lugosi, Mortabel, Nightshade, Rasputin, Sanguine, von Pitt, von Tyn
- **[RS3]:** vampyres have their own alphabet; their naming draws on Romanian and Russian; they likely adopted Infernal (Latin) as Zarosians.
- **Can the player understand it?** There is nothing to understand. Vampyres speak the common tongue.
- **Sprinkling:** vampyres should **not** speak in vampyric words. Use their naming style, Latin-flavoured roots (sanguis, nox, mors — the wiki notes these real etymologies), and lore terms such as *juvinate*, *vyre*, *blood tithe*. Those terms are English-language lore jargon, not vampyric words.

## 9. VPSG code (in the category, but a cipher)
The Varrock Palace Secret Guard's 26-letter cipher of warped Latin letters. It appears in What Lies Below: Rat Burgiss's letter to Surok Magis, and the beacon ring from Zaff. It is written only and has no vocabulary, so it is not useful for speech.

## Quick takeaways for the plugin
1. **Best for sprinkling:** **TzHaar** (rich vocabulary, real in-game pattern of broken common speech plus TzHaar words, player address rules), **Varlamore** interjections (Nilsal, Timoiva, Iknami, Kuani, Tetamo, designed exactly for this), **Gnomish** (big lexicon, `-olly` insult suffix, glider place names).
2. **Babble-only with an understanding gate:** **Monkeys** (exact phrase generator; plain English if MM2 is done or the M'speak amulet is worn) and **ghosts** ("Woooo"; Ghostspeak amulet).
3. **Short fixed phrases, no meanings:** **Skavid** (Tanath cur!, Bidith tanath..., Gor nod...).
4. **Quote only, rarely spoken:** **Dragonkin** (shrine text), **Old Ones** (ruins and scholars; Eyatlalli's "iknil").
5. **Nothing to sprinkle; do not invent words:** **Lunar** (alphabet only; Moon Clan speak the common tongue; the seal is a pass, not a translator), **Vampyre** (no attested words), Fremennik, Elvish (Welsh-flavoured names only), Mahjarrat.
